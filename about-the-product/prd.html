Product Requirements Document

ORVEXA

An AI-powered coordination layer that helps residents instantly access and follow building-specific bylaws and rules.

Varun Sagar KPropTech · AI (RAG)October 2026

TL;DR In high-rise communities, bylaws and operational policies change often, so residents struggle to track and interpret them and lean on building management or the concierge for answers. That creates bottlenecks, delays and sometimes fines. ORVEXA is an AI-native building operating system, powered by RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation), that answers residents' questions from the building's own, up-to-date documents and shows the exact rule, its source and its date with every answer. When the evidence is weak, it does not guess: it shows a disclaimer and hands the question to the concierge. Grounding every answer in the building's approved documents, rather than a general chatbot, is what keeps answers accurate and verifiable. Success is measured with precision@K, context recall, faithfulness, escalation rate and CSAT.

Use case 1 — Bylaw and rule retrieval

Happy path: a resident instantly retrieves the latest bylaws, rules and FAQs and makes an informed decision without contacting management.

Edge case, stale data: the system retrieves an outdated bylaw version. Example: a resident asks whether an after-hours move-in with small furniture is exempt from fees; the system cites an older version with no fee instead of the latest council-approved update that enforces fees regardless.

Use case 2 — Visitor parking after-hours (policy interpretation and abuse awareness)

Happy path: a resident expecting a visitor after concierge hours verifies visitor parking rules, allowable duration and restrictions so the guest complies.

Edge case, policy abuse: a resident queries daily to get around visitor parking limits, despite the rule restricting 2 consecutive overnight stays.

Low confidence or misuse. If the system detects an unclear enforcement interpretation, a policy conflict across documents, or repeated loophole-seeking intent, it must: prevent ambiguous answers that read as loopholes; escalate repeated or suspicious usage to building management or the concierge; and avoid helping optimize rules beyond their intended design.

Section 4

Failure modes

01Bad source data: old facts

Breaks: old facts get indexed, so the model answers with superseded policies, fees, dates or procedures.

Detect: compare "published at" and "modified at" against a freshness policy, e.g. "policy docs older than 30 days are stale unless revalidated."

Fix: keep only the latest valid version, or keep older versions marked inactive or archived.

02Bad source data: duplicates

Breaks: the same content appears multiple times, making retrieval noisy.

Detect: compare metadata (title, URL, source ID), then content hashes for exact duplicates, and near-duplicate similarity for slightly changed copies.

Fix: deduplicate before chunking and keep one canonical copy; if two versions are both useful, merge them into one source-of-truth record.

03Wrong chunking

Breaks: bylaws, sentences or rules are split mid-thought, so answers are incomplete or misleading.

Detect: user feedback (NPS or reports mentioning "answer incomplete" or "missing rule"); a small test suite of whole-rule, edge-case and negative queries; recheck embedding choices.

Fix: chunk on semantic boundaries (headers, paragraphs, numbered lists, sentences); keep each bylaw intact; overlap 50–150 tokens to carry context; always show the original rule with the answer; show confidence scores and warnings.

04Retrieval misses

Breaks: the right document exists, but the system doesn't find it and answers from weak or unrelated context.

Detect: retrieval logs show low recall or wrong top results for important queries.

Fix: better metadata and indexing; synonyms, aliases and query rewriting; re-ranking so the best passage rises to the top.

05The LLM hallucinates anyway

Breaks: even with the right context, the model invents details or connects facts that aren't written there.

Detect: citations don't support the generated statements; evaluation tests show ungrounded answers.

Fix: answer only from retrieved context; stricter system prompt (if the answer isn't in the sources, say so, point the resident to building management and notify the concierge); require citations for every factual claim.

06Knowledge goes stale

Breaks: answers that were once correct are now outdated: bylaws, rules and fines.

Detect: documents with old and new timestamps and version numbers; users report answers that conflict with current policy.

Fix: store version and timestamp metadata and prefer the newest valid version; mark old versions inactive; re-ingest on a schedule (bi-weekly or monthly) after council meetings; show the document date in the answer.

07Users misuse it

Breaks: users push it outside its scope to find loopholes.

Detect: attempts to make the model take decisions it shouldn't; off-topic or unsafe queries; repeated queries relying on it for tasks that need management or concierge review.

Fix: clear scope boundaries; input filters for unsafe or out-of-scope requests; "not a final authority" labels; management approval before action in high-stakes cases.

08Free AI quota runs out mid-question

Breaks: each question needs several model calls (embed, re-rank, answer, faithfulness check); if the free-tier daily limit is hit partway, an answer could arrive without its check.

Detect: model-call errors and quota responses in the per-call logs.

Fix: if any model call fails, reply "can't answer right now, please contact the concierge" and escalate; never return an answer that skipped the faithfulness check. Keep the daily per-resident query limit inside the free quota.

Build brief

Inputs

Knowledge base: bylaws, rules and concierge FAQs, as PDFs up to 50 MB each, refreshed monthly.

Model rules

  • Answer only from the building's retrieved documents; label each document piece as reference text, not instructions.
  • Every response includes citations, the supporting excerpt, its timestamp and a source link.
  • When the evidence is weak: show a disclaimer, avoid definitive answers, notify the concierge and create a dashboard escalation.
